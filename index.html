About

My start in game development goes back to 2009 when I coded my first project for a middle school science fair, sparking a lifelong fascination with how games work under the hood. What draws me most to the medium is creating unique systems: mechanics that bend or blur genres, and design that rewards curiosity and allows for player expression through gameplay. That same love of emergent design pulls me toward roguelikes and indie studios, which have more freedom to break conventions and take creative risks.

I especially love the way games build community around the unpredictability of emergent play, whether it's a raid group coordinating in real time or a table improvising through a shared story. To me, games are an artistic medium in their own right, capable of pushing boundaries through branching narratives, interactive animation, and dynamic audio. I approach design with an inventive yet practical mindset, informed by direct experience across both art and engineering pipelines. Over the past decade, I've pursued this craft through a blend of academic study and self-directed practice: teaching myself open source development tools and diving deep into systems design and theory.
